# Enquiry to Quotation — phase-wise plan

**Business outcome.** A buyer at a trading company can take a customer enquiry, compare supplier offers on a common basis, and approve a customer quotation with a clear reason for each supplier choice and selling price.

## Phase 1 — Define the trading scenario (complete)

[Approved Oman-only scenario and decision rules](phase-1-trading-scenario.md)

- Use one fictional Oman-based distributor, one product family (industrial fasteners), a small set of suppliers, and one named buyer and approver.
- Agree what a customer request must specify, including quantity, unit, critical specifications, quote deadline, and required delivery date when given.
- Agree what makes an offer usable. Set policies for brand and origin, acceptable substitutions, minimum purchase quantities, lead time, and offer expiry.
- Agree how the buyer compares delivered OMR offers and when pricing below the margin floor needs approval.
- Decide when the buyer can quote part of an enquiry and how to communicate what is still pending.

**Done when:** The buyer and approver can walk through one enquiry on paper and agree what they would quote, what they would hold, and why.

## Phase 2 — Prepare synthetic business documents

[Reusable trading fixtures](trading-fixtures/)

- Write customer emails and create typed requirement PDFs with believable item descriptions, specifications, quantities, units, and dates.
- Create fictional supplier profiles and supplier reply emails with completed Excel templates. Include delivered OMR prices, box and piece pricing, minimum quantities, different validity dates, and competing lead times.
- Create past supplier offers, customer quotations, and confirmed purchase records. Include alternate descriptions of the same item, near-identical descriptions with different specs, and older prices that should carry less weight.
- Include cases where an offer covers only some items, a supplier revises a price, a substitute brand is proposed, or a pack size is missing.
- Write an answer key for each case covering valid matches, comparable costs, exceptions, recommended suppliers, and expected quote coverage. Set aside fresh cases for the final evaluation.

- Replay standalone email events in chronological order. Each message contains only the information the sender had at that point. Use local files until the end-to-end workflow is ready for Gmail integration.
- Vary document layouts, wording, and commercial terms. Include realistic failures such as missing attachments, incorrect workbook references, and ambiguous pack sizes.

**Done when:** Each scenario has realistic source documents and an agreed expected outcome, and the evaluation set is separate from the practice cases.

## Phase 3 — Take the enquiry through supplier outreach

- Check the customer's requested items, quantities, specs, and dates against the original email and attachment. Resolve missing or conflicting details before asking for prices.
- Identify suitable suppliers for each line using their product coverage and relevant sourcing history.
- Prepare supplier requests that state the items and commercial terms the buyer needs back. Give each request a reference the buyer can use when replies arrive.
- Have the buyer verify the supplier selection and the contents of each draft. Keep the customer identity, internal customer references, and resale prices confidential.

**Done when:** The buyer can approve and manually send supplier requests that accurately reflect the confirmed enquiry.

## Phase 4 — Compare offers and recommend suppliers

- Match supplier descriptions to requested items even when the wording differs. Send uncertain matches and critical spec conflicts to the buyer for review.
- Compare each offer in OMR per requested unit. Show the original delivered price and the pack conversion.
- Show minimum order quantity, actual purchase spend, lead time, validity, brand, and origin alongside price. Flag missing or expired terms that prevent a decision.
- Compare prices against relevant history on the same cost basis. Flag unusual prices and substitute brands with the evidence the buyer needs to judge them.
- Recommend a supplier per line and explain why. Make it clear when an apparently cheaper offer misses a requirement or costs more because of minimum quantities. Mark lines with no acceptable offer as blocked.

**Done when:** The buyer can explain why each recommendation is usable, review any exception, and identify the lines still preventing a complete quote.

## Phase 5 — Approve the quotation and carry history forward

- Confirm the selected supplier and current offer for each quoted line. Use multiple suppliers when that meets the customer's needs.
- Apply the agreed selling-price and margin rules. Route pricing or substitution exceptions to the approver.
- Check that the customer's quote validity fits the supporting offers. For a partial quotation, make it clear which items are offered and which remain pending.
- Prepare the customer quotation for staff approval and manual sending.
- Keep supplier offers, issued customer quotations, and confirmed purchases separate in history. Record an actual purchase only once it is confirmed.

**Done when:** Staff can approve a complete or clearly marked partial quote, and a later enquiry can distinguish a historical offer from a price actually paid.

## Phase 6 — Evaluate the full business workflow

- Run the workflow on the held-out cases using their original synthetic documents. Check results against the answer key.
- Include a straightforward case and cases where a near-match has the wrong spec, an apparently cheap offer becomes costly after normalisation, or a revised reply changes the supplier choice.
- Check item matches, comparable costs, supplier recommendations, quote coverage, and customer confidentiality.
- Record how many cases were correct, needed review, or produced an incorrect outcome. Fix failures that would result in a wrong item, wrong price, or unsupported delivery promise, then rerun the affected cases.

**Done when:** We can show the results and case counts, explain why staff review was needed, and identify any remaining limitations.

## Phase 7 — Package the portfolio case study

- Write a short case study covering the trader's problem, the buyer's decisions, the scope, and the evaluation results.
- Record a walkthrough from the original enquiry to the approved quote. Include one consequential exception and show how it changed the recommendation or quote.
- Include a representative synthetic requirement PDF, supplier replies, per-line comparison, and final quotation so prospects can follow the evidence.
- Label the case study as self-directed and based on synthetic data. Credit the public Upwork brief as inspiration and report measured results from the evaluation.

**Done when:** A prospective client can understand the workflow, inspect the evidence behind a sourcing decision, and see what the portfolio project demonstrated.

## Project completion

The project is portfolio-ready when all seven phases are complete and the synthetic documents, working workflow, evaluation results, and published case study describe the same end-to-end outcome.
